# PSY2 — the Air France Cadets final selection: what it really is

_Research deliverable for US-110 (GitHub #72). Feeds US-111 (interview question bank & self-recording)
and US-112 (group-exercise guide & self-assessment) of EPIC-11._
_Research done 2026-09-13. All URLs were accessed on that date unless stated otherwise._

## How to read this document

Same confidence scale as `psy0-spec.md`:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[confirmed]** | Stated by an official Air France source (corporate site, FAQ, job offer, press release). |
| **[reported]** | Consistent candidate feedback (Aeronet forum debriefs) and/or prep vendors (Pilotest, France Prépa, Test-Pilote…). Reliable for structure and themes, less so for exact wording or numbers. |
| **[assumed]** | Our best guess where nothing precise was found. |

**Headline findings (TL;DR)**

1. PSY2 is the **third and final stage**, held **from January** of the campaign year at the **Air France selection department, Roissy-CDG**, over roughly **two days**. It has three components: **two personality inventories**, **one group exercise**, and **individual interviews with psychologists and pilots**. **[confirmed]** structure (per `psy0-spec.md` §1, S1); **[reported]** two-day duration and day-by-day layout.
2. The **group exercise is explicitly under confidentiality** — candidates sign an agreement not to disclose its content — and **Air France itself states that paid coaching targeting the group exercise "n'a démontré aucune plus-value"** during its selections. **[confirmed]** (S1, echoed by multiple prep vendors). This document therefore describes only the *type* and *evaluation criteria* of the exercise, never its content, in line with `psy0-spec.md` §7's "format vs content" rule.
3. The **personality inventories are not named by Air France**, and no candidate debrief we found reliably names the instrument(s). Vendor pages mention SOSIE and TD12 as possibilities but do not confirm them as the AF PSY2 tools, and other sources speculate NEO-PI-R; treat all of this as **[open question]** — the app must not claim a specific test name.
4. The **individual interview** is reported as **two separate sessions** (with pilots, and with a psychologist), each covering **motivation/background, CRM-adjacent themes (teamwork, hierarchy, stress), and — with the pilots — a short round of PPL-level aeronautical knowledge**. Total time across both sessions is reported around **50–60 minutes** combined, with a short English component. **[reported]**
5. Outcome is a **commission de recrutement** verdict: **"réussite"** or **"ajournement"** — 1 ajournement → one more PSY2-only attempt after a delay (~1 year, reported), 2 ajournements → back to the start of the whole process (per `psy0-spec.md` §1 retake table, PSY2-specific delay is **[reported]**, not spelled out in the same place as PSY0/PSY1 rules).
6. Consequence for the kanban: US-111 and US-112 should build a **theme-based interview question bank + self-recording tool** and a **CRM-behaviour group-exercise guide + self-assessment checklist**, never a leaked-exercise simulator. See §4.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 2. PSY2 in detail

### 2.1 Overall structure and logistics

| Item | Finding | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Air France's own selection department at **Roissy-Charles-de-Gaulle** airport (not ENAC, not a hotel — an AF internal facility). | **[confirmed]** S1 for "Roissy-CDG, service sélection Air France"; exact building/logistics **[reported]** |
| When | "En début d'année prochaine" — PSY2 sessions run from **January** through the spring, scheduled individually per candidate as convocations go out (candidates report convocations arriving months after a PSY1 pass, e.g. PSY1 passed Oct 2023 → PSY2 convocation late May 2024 → session held July 2024). | **[confirmed]** timing window; **[reported]** the multi-month gap and rolling scheduling (P5) |
| Duration | Candidate and vendor sources describe **two days**: day 1 = personality inventory (computer-based, ~2 h) + group exercises (an afternoon); day 2 = individual interviews. Some accounts describe a shorter one-day format depending on campaign/session; treat the two-day layout as the common case. | **[reported]** P1, P2, P4 |
| Dress code | No official document specifies a dress code; no candidate debrief we found states one explicitly either. Community norm for any French recruitment "entretien" of this seriousness is business/smart-casual attire; treat as **inferred convention**, not a documented rule. | **[open question]** |
| What to bring | Not documented publicly beyond the standard convocation letter requirements (ID, any documents AF requests). | **[open question]** |
| Confidentiality | Candidates **sign a confidentiality agreement** before or around the group exercise, covering its content and procedure. This is the basis for Air France's own "no added value from paid coaching" position and for our own legal/ethics stance (§5). | **[confirmed]** S1 (existence of the confidentiality framing); **[reported]** the signature step itself (P2) |
| Groups | Candidates are assigned to a small cohort for the day, meeting each other around midday before the afternoon group exercise(s). | **[reported]** P6 |

### 2.2 The personality inventories

| Item | Finding | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Count / modality | **Two personality inventories**, administered by **computer**, reported to take roughly **2 hours** combined, based on situational-judgment-style items ("comment réagiriez-vous si…") as well as trait/preference items. | **[confirmed]** "two inventories" exist (S1); **[reported]** modality/duration (P1, P4) |
| Instrument names | **Not confirmed.** Some prep-vendor marketing copy references "SOSIE" and "TD12" in proximity to PSY2 prep offers, and one older forum thread title mentions "NEO PI-R" without confirming AF uses it for PSY2 specifically. No candidate debrief we found states with confidence which instrument(s) Air France uses today. **Do not present any named instrument as confirmed in the app.** | **[open question]** |
| Purpose (as understood) | Establish a **personality/behavioural profile** used (a) to prepare the psychologist's questions in the individual interview and (b) to check **coherence** between the profile and how the candidate actually behaves in the group exercise and interview. Several debriefs mention the psychologist "debriefing" the inventory results with the candidate, described as generally pleasant/complimentary in tone. | **[reported]** P1, P3, P6 |
| Coaching stance | Community and vendor consensus: **do not try to game a personality inventory** — answer honestly; consistency between the inventory, the group exercise and the interview is itself part of what is evaluated. This aligns with Air France's own framing of the exercise as seeking "authenticity, not learned behaviours." | **[reported]** P2 |

### 2.3 The group exercise

Per Air France's own confidentiality framing (S1) and the ethics rule inherited from `psy0-spec.md` §7, **no exercise content, wording, prompts, scenario text or "trick" is reproduced here**. Only the reported *type*, *duration*, *group size* and *evaluated behaviours* are described — the same restraint the psy0-spec applied to real test items.

| Item | Finding | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Confidentiality stance | Air France explicitly states that **paid coaching aimed at the group exercise has demonstrated no added value** ("n'a démontré aucune plus-value") in its selections, and frames the exercise as looking for **authentic behaviour**, not rehearsed technique. Value of preparation, per Air France's own framing, lies elsewhere: cognitive skills, English, aeronautical culture, and the strength of the candidate's project. | **[confirmed]** S1 |
| Format (type only) | Candidate debriefs describe an afternoon built around **small-group collective problem-solving under time pressure** — accounts vary between a single extended task and two to three shorter activities (e.g. a short current-affairs mini-debate, a document-based analysis and discussion, and a collective logic/resource puzzle where each participant holds partial information that must be pooled). We report the *range* of formats seen across debriefs rather than asserting one fixed structure, since sessions appear to vary by campaign. | **[reported]** P2, P6 |
| Duration / group size | Individual activities reported in the **10–40 minute** range each, filling a full afternoon; small groups (a handful of candidates, consistent with the cohort assigned that morning). | **[reported]** P2, P6 |
| What is evaluated (CRM/behavioural) | Debriefs and vendor material converge on three clusters that map directly onto **CRM (Crew Resource Management) behaviours**: (a) **analysis/decision-making** — attention management, reasoning quality, composure under stress, reactivity, appropriate risk-taking, autonomy, precision; (b) **relational/communication skills** — natural communication, clarity, interpersonal warmth, adaptability, self-assertion without dominating, awareness of "authority gradient" (how one manages disagreement with others, including more senior-seeming peers); (c) **professional fit** — rule-following, catching and correcting one's own errors, intellectual honesty, and basic company knowledge surfacing naturally in discussion. Time management of the group as a whole is also explicitly watched. | **[reported]** P2, consistent with P1's jury description |
| A recurring interview follow-up | Several debriefs report the individual interview later asks candidates to **reflect on their own performance in the group exercise** (what they would do differently), testing self-critique and humility rather than re-testing the exercise itself. | **[reported]** P2 |

### 2.4 The individual interview(s)

| Item | Finding | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Reported as **two separate individual sessions**: one with **line pilots** (often described as closer to a classic job interview: background, motivation, knowledge of the training and the company, current affairs in aviation), and one with an **occupational psychologist** (closer to the personality inventory: self-awareness, ability to name and own one's weaknesses, coherence between the inventory results and the day's observed behaviour). Some sources describe a single panel-style session with a 3-person jury instead of two fully separate ones — both patterns recur across debriefs and may reflect different campaigns/sites. | **[reported]** P1, P2, P4 |
| Jury composition | A **panel of about three people**: an Air France line pilot (often a captain), a human-resources representative, and an occupational psychologist — each applying their own evaluation angle (technical/organisational/psychological). | **[reported]** P1 |
| Duration | Combined interview time reported around **50–60 minutes**, structured loosely as: an unprompted personal presentation (5–10 min), a longer structured interview (~30 min), and a short round of technical/aeronautical questions (~10 min); plus a short English-language component (~10 min) in at least some accounts. | **[reported]** P1 |
| Language | Primarily **French**; an **English** segment is reported (a short oral exchange), consistent with Air France's general emphasis on English throughout the process. | **[reported]** P1, P3 |
| Themes reported at the theme level (no verbatim leaks) | Career motivation and why aviation; why Air France specifically (vs. other operators or the airline pilot "pro" track); coherence and realism of the candidate's career project and training-level justification; learning from failure / handling a past setback; self-awareness — naming and owning a weakness in a cockpit-relevant way; handling disagreement with a hierarchical superior or a crewmate; stress and pressure management; reacting to a difficult or fast decision; understanding of CRM concepts (teamwork, communication, workload sharing) even without formal training; a 5–10 year vision of the career; PPL-level aeronautical knowledge (e.g. basic meteorology terms, radio/nav concepts, crosswind limits) with the pilots specifically; and — reflectively — what the candidate would change about their own group-exercise performance. | **[reported]** synthesised from P1, P2, P3, P4, P6 |
| What is *not* tested here | No frozen-ATPL-level technical exam; no repeat of the PSY0/PSY1 cognitive batteries. The psychologist session in particular is explicitly **not** described as a technical interview. | **[reported]** P4 |
| Debrief practice | Candidates who fail PSY2 report receiving a **written/oral debrief from a psychologist of the recruitment service**, going through positive and negative notes — but only after a **first** ajournement (not automatically at every stage, and not for a second definitive rejection in the same way). | **[reported]** P3, consistent with `psy0-spec.md`'s own S1-derived rule that a written debrief follows a first PSY2 ajournement |

### 2.5 The recruitment commission and ajournement rules

| Item | Finding | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Commission | The **commission de recrutement** reviews the full PSY2 file (personality inventories, group-exercise observations, interview notes) and renders a verdict: **success** or **ajournement**. | **[confirmed]** S1 |
| Ajournement (1st) | A first ajournement at PSY2 allows **one further attempt at PSY2 only** (the candidate does not redo PSY0/PSY1), after a waiting period reported around **one year** — some sources describe deferral windows as configurable per phase rather than a fixed universal delay; treat the exact delay as approximate. Candidates report receiving a **written/psychologist debrief** after this first ajournement. | **[confirmed]** existence of the "1 year → PSY2 only again" rule (S1, `psy0-spec.md` §1); **[reported]** exact delay length and debrief practice |
| Ajournement (2nd) | A second ajournement (or exhausting the single retake) results in **starting the entire process over** (back through PSY0), reported at a **~2–3 year** cooling-off horizon depending on the source consulted. | **[confirmed]** the "2 years → full process again" structure (S1); **[reported]** the precise multi-year figure (varies 2–3 years across sources) |
| Accepting an offer | Successful candidates are expected to accept the assigned training start date; deferring a training start once accepted is not generally available — candidates should expect **no flexibility** once a class-1 medical and training slot are confirmed. | **[reported]** P4 |

## 4. App scope (input for US-111 and US-112)

Guiding constraint, repeated from §5: **no reproduction of the group exercise's content, no personality-test "answer key," and no claim that the app can predict or game a real evaluator's decision.** Everything below is framed as **self-knowledge and skills practice**, matching Air France's own stated position that authenticity — not rehearsed material — is what is evaluated.

### 4.1 US-111 — Interview preparation: question bank & self-recording

**What it should contain**

- A **question bank organised by theme** (below), each question **original wording**, written by us from the theme-level findings in §2.4 — never copied from any prep vendor or forum verbatim quote.
- For each question: **model-answer guidance** (what a strong answer covers, e.g. "name a concrete situation, your action, the outcome, what you'd repeat/change" — i.e. STAR-shaped guidance without mandating the acronym), not a scripted answer.
- A **timer + audio self-recording** feature: candidate picks a question, sees a short prep countdown, records a timed spoken answer, can play it back.
- **Personal notes** attached to each question/recording (what went well, what to change next time).
- A **self-assessment rubric** to score one's own recorded answer (see indicators below) — self-scored only, no simulated grading of "pass/fail."
- Optional: a short **English-language** sub-mode for a handful of questions, reflecting the reported English segment of the interview.

**Proposed theme list with ~10 example questions per theme** (all original wording; theme-level, matching §2.4's reported themes)

**Theme A — Motivation and career project**
1. What first drew you to becoming a pilot, and how has that reason evolved since?
2. Why Air France (or Transavia) specifically, rather than another operator or track?
3. Walk me through your path to this interview — what does it show about you?
4. What alternative career did you seriously consider, and why didn't you choose it?
5. Where do you see yourself professionally in ten years?
6. What part of the pilot's job do you expect to find hardest, day to day?
7. What have you done in the last twelve months to actively prepare for this career?
8. If you are not selected today, what would you do next?
9. What does "being a professional" mean to you in an aviation context?
10. What would your entourage say is your strongest reason for pursuing this, beyond passion?

**Theme B — Background and self-presentation**
1. Present your background in a few minutes, without reading notes.
2. Which past experience — inside or outside aviation — best explains who you are today?
3. What is a decision from your studies or career you would make differently now?
4. Describe a moment you had to learn something difficult quickly. How did you approach it?
5. What achievement are you proudest of, and what did it cost you to get there?
6. Tell me about a time you changed your mind about something important after new information.
7. What is a skill you've built entirely on your own initiative?
8. How would a former teacher or supervisor describe you in three words, and why?
9. What is the biggest gap between how you see yourself and how others describe you?
10. What non-aviation experience do you think will make you a better crew member?

**Theme C — CRM and teamwork**
1. Describe a time you disagreed with someone in a position of authority. What did you do?
2. Tell me about a time a team you were part of nearly failed. What was your role in the recovery?
3. How do you make sure a quieter team member's input gets heard?
4. Describe a time you had to give difficult feedback to a peer.
5. What do you do when you realise, mid-task, that you made a mistake others haven't noticed?
6. How would you handle a colleague who consistently cuts corners on a shared procedure?
7. Tell me about a time you had to follow a decision you privately disagreed with.
8. What does "good communication under pressure" look like to you, concretely?
9. Describe a time you had to coordinate several people with conflicting priorities.
10. How do you build trust quickly with people you've just met and must rely on?

**Theme D — Stress and pressure management**
1. Describe the most stressful situation you've faced and how you got through it.
2. What physical or mental signs tell you that you're becoming overloaded?
3. Tell me about a time you had to make a fast decision with incomplete information.
4. How do you prepare yourself before a high-stakes event (exam, competition, performance)?
5. Describe a time stress caused you to make a mistake. What did you change afterward?
6. What do you do in the minutes right after something goes wrong, before acting?
7. How do you separate an urgent problem from an important one when both appear at once?
8. Tell me about a deadline you nearly missed. What went wrong and what did you learn?
9. What routines help you recover after a demanding day?
10. Describe a situation where you had to stay calm while someone near you was not.

**Theme E — Self-awareness and weaknesses**
1. What is a weakness you are actively working on, and what have you done about it?
2. Describe a piece of critical feedback that was hard to hear but useful.
3. What kind of situation brings out the worst in you, and how do you manage it?
4. If your closest friend had to name your biggest blind spot, what would it be?
5. Tell me about a time your first instinct in a situation turned out to be wrong.
6. What have you changed about how you work as a result of a past failure?
7. How do you know when you're wrong in an argument, and what do you do about it?
8. What kind of feedback do you actively ask for, and from whom?
9. Describe a habit you've deliberately built to compensate for a known weakness.
10. What would make you a difficult crew member to fly with, honestly?

**Theme F — Aviation and company knowledge**
1. In your own words, what is CRM and why does it matter operationally?
2. What do you know about Air France's / Transavia's fleet and route network?
3. What is the difference between a METAR and a TAF, and when would you use each?
4. Explain, simply, why crosswind matters for takeoff and landing.
5. What does "authority gradient" mean in a cockpit, and why can it be a risk?
6. What recent aviation news story caught your attention, and why?
7. What do you understand about how a multi-crew cockpit divides tasks?
8. Why does a strict rest/duty-time regulation exist for pilots?
9. What is icing, and why is it operationally significant?
10. What does the cadet training pathway involve, month by month, as you understand it?

**Theme G — Reflective / meta questions**
1. Looking back at today, what would you change about how you approached the group exercise?
2. What did today's exercises reveal about you that a resume can't show?
3. What is one thing you'll do differently before your next assessment, regardless of today's result?
4. If we asked the people you worked with today to describe you in one sentence, what would they say?
5. What part of today felt most natural to you, and what part felt most forced?
6. What question do you wish we had asked you today?
7. What is the one thing you most want us to remember about you?
8. How did the personality-inventory results match how you actually behaved today?
9. What would you tell a friend preparing for this same day, based on what you just experienced?
10. If you don't succeed this time, what would you want written in your debrief?

**Self-assessment rubric (per recorded answer)** — candidate scores 1–4 (weak/developing/solid/strong) on: *structure* (clear situation → action → result / reasoning), *concreteness* (specific example vs. generic claim), *self-awareness* (owns a real weakness or mistake, not a disguised strength), *relevance to aviation/CRM* (connects the answer to teamwork, safety or professional judgement where natural), *delivery* (pace, clarity, confidence without notes).

### 4.2 US-112 — Group exercise guide & self-assessment

**What it should contain**

- **Lessons on CRM behaviours and group dynamics** — conceptual content only (definitions, why each behaviour matters operationally), never a rehearsal of any specific exercise.
- A **CRM behaviour rubric** (below) usable both as study material and as a **self-assessment checklist after a mock session**.
- Guidance on **how to organise peer mock groups**: recommended group size (3–5), a generic, original mock task (e.g. a logic/resource-sharing puzzle authored by us, or a short current-affairs mini-debate prompt) used purely as a vehicle to practise the *behaviours*, explicit framing that the mock task is **not** a simulation of the real exercise, a suggested timebox (10–40 min per round, matching reported ranges), and a rotation so each participant also observes and rates others using the same rubric.
- A short module on the **"authenticity over technique"** framing, restating Air France's own position, so users don't waste effort trying to find "the trick."

**CRM behaviour rubric** (six dimensions, each with observable indicators; 1–4 scale per dimension)

| Dimension | What it is | Observable indicators (what an observer/self-rater looks for) |
|---|---|---|
| **Communication** | Sharing information clearly, checking understanding, speaking up when something matters | States information precisely and audibly; confirms others received/understood a key point; asks clarifying questions instead of assuming; avoids talking over others; summarises where the group stands at key moments |
| **Leadership / followership** | Taking initiative appropriately, but also supporting someone else's lead without dominating | Proposes a way forward when the group stalls; invites quieter members to contribute; defers to a good idea from someone else without ego; does not monopolise the task; adapts role between leading and supporting as the situation requires |
| **Situational awareness** | Tracking the state of the task, the time, and the group as a whole | Notices when the group is off-track or repeating itself; tracks remaining time and flags it; notices when a piece of information contradicts an earlier one; keeps attention on the whole picture, not just their own sub-piece |
| **Decision-making** | Making a reasoned call, especially under incomplete information or time pressure | Names the options considered, not just the conclusion; distinguishes a reversible choice from a risky one; commits to a decision once made instead of re-litigating; revises a decision cleanly when new information invalidates it |
| **Workload management** | Distributing effort and attention so nothing critical is dropped | Suggests splitting sub-tasks logically; checks nothing important was left unassigned; avoids getting stuck on a low-value detail at the expense of the overall goal; asks for help before being overwhelmed |
| **Teamwork** | General cooperative behaviour and group cohesion | Builds on others' contributions rather than restarting from scratch; acknowledges good points made by others by name; manages disagreement respectfully; helps the group recover after an error without blame; contributes to a positive, task-focused atmosphere |

**Self-assessment checklist (after a mock session)** — for each of the six dimensions: one thing done well (specific instance), one thing to improve next time (specific instance), and a 1–4 self-rating; plus two free-text prompts: "What would I do differently if this were the real thing?" and "What did I learn about my default behaviour under pressure?"

## 5. Open questions

1. **Exact name(s) of the two personality inventories.** No official or reliably-sourced candidate account confirms a specific instrument (SOSIE/TD12/NEO-PI-R and others surfaced only in ambiguous vendor copy). Do not name one in the app.
2. **Exact current group-exercise format** (number of distinct activities, whether it's a single extended task or several short ones, precise durations) — debriefs vary by campaign/session and Air France's confidentiality stance limits what candidates can legally publish, so triangulating an exact 2026-era format may never be possible; the app should describe *categories* of exercise, not a fixed script.
3. **Precise ajournement delay lengths** (1st ajournement wait before PSY2 retake; 2nd ajournement wait before restarting at PSY0) — sources give approximate ranges (~1 year / ~2–3 years) rather than a codified rule as crisp as the PSY0/PSY1 "3 failures / 2 failures" rules in `psy0-spec.md`.
4. **Whether PSY2 is one day or two** for a given campaign — both patterns appear in debriefs; may depend on session/site or have changed over time.
5. **Dress code and logistics detail** (what the convocation letter actually specifies) — not found in any public source.
6. **Whether the interview is one panel session or two separate ones** (pilots vs. psychologist) — both patterns reported; may vary by campaign.
7. **How personality-inventory results factor numerically (if at all) into the commission's decision**, versus being purely a discussion aid for the interview.
8. **Whether Transavia-track PSY2 differs from Air France-track PSY2** — not addressed by our sources; assumed identical given the shared Cadet program framing in `psy0-spec.md`.

## 7. Legal and ethics note

- **Unofficial.** As in `psy0-spec.md` §7: this project is an independent training aid, not affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to Air France, Transavia, ENAC, or their contractors.
- **No reproduction of group-exercise content.** Air France states candidates are bound by confidentiality around the PSY2 group exercise and that paid coaching targeting it brings **no added value**. Accordingly this document, and any content built from it, describe only the **type** of exercise and the **behavioural/CRM criteria** it evaluates — never a scenario, prompt, dataset, or "solution" that could resemble the real task. US-112 must not ship a "simulated group exercise" that imitates a leaked format; original, clearly-labelled mock tasks used purely to rehearse CRM behaviours are fine.
- **No personality-test gaming.** The app must never present a "correct" way to answer a personality inventory, must not name or imitate a specific commercial instrument, and must frame all personality-related content as **self-knowledge** (understanding one's own tendencies) rather than as a way to produce a target profile. This also matches candidate/vendor consensus that coherence between the inventory and observed behaviour is itself evaluated — "gaming" the inventory is self-defeating even on its own terms.
- **Interview content is safe to build on.** Unlike the group exercise, the individual interview is not described anywhere as confidential; question *themes* (never exact leaked wording, which we also avoid by writing our own questions) are fair game, matching how `psy0-spec.md` treats PSY0 test *formats* as reusable while treating specific items as not reusable.
- **No outcome prediction.** As with PSY0/PSY1, the app must not claim that self-assessment scores predict success at the real PSY2, and must repeat the standard unofficial/estimative disclaimer (see `psy0-spec.md` §7 for the FR/EN wording, reused verbatim for PSY2 screens).
- **Data.** Self-recorded audio and self-assessment notes are sensitive personal reflections; store them locally by default, never upload without explicit consent, and let users delete recordings easily.
